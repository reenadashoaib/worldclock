      Using Hourline to plan across time zones

      Every row shows a city's local time right now, how far ahead or behind you it is, and whether it is already tomorrow or still yesterday there. Cities are listed from the furthest west to the furthest east, so you can read the list like a map of the day moving around the planet.

      To check a city that is not on your list, type it into the search at the top of the page. It covers every city and town with 15,000 or more people, and shows the time there as you type. Choose a result to see its date and clock, and add it to your list if you need it again.

      To find a meeting time, drag the slider under your clock. Every city moves with it. When the rows you care about are all in daytime blue, you have found a time that works for everyone.

      Daylight saving time

      Many countries move their clocks forward one hour in spring and back in autumn, but they do not all change on the same date. The northern and southern hemispheres change in opposite directions, and many places near the equator never change at all. Hourline uses your browser's time zone database, so clocks update for daylight saving automatically.

      Questions

      Why do some cities show half-hour differences?

      Some time zones are offset from UTC by 30 or 45 minutes. India runs at UTC+5:30, Nepal at UTC+5:45, and Newfoundland in Canada at UTC−3:30.

      Where is my city list saved?

      Only in this browser on this device. Nothing about your list is sent to us. Clearing your browser data resets it to the default cities.

      How accurate are the clocks?

      They use your device's clock. If your device time is wrong, every city will be off by the same amount.
