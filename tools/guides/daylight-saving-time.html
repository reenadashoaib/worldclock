Daylight saving time is the practice of moving clocks forward by one hour in spring and back by one hour in autumn. The idea is to shift an hour of daylight from the early morning, when many people are asleep, to the evening, when they are awake to use it. About a third of the world's countries change their clocks this way. The rest keep the same time all year.

Who changes their clocks

Daylight saving time is mostly used far from the equator, where the length of the day changes a lot between summer and winter. In practice that means:

  • most of the United States and Canada;
  • the United Kingdom and every country in the European Union;
  • parts of Australia, including New South Wales, Victoria and Tasmania;
  • New Zealand, and a few other countries in the Middle East and South America.

Most of Asia and Africa do not use it at all. Japan, China, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Indonesia all keep the same time all year. Several countries have also given it up in recent years: Türkiye in 2016, Brazil in 2019 and Mexico in 2022 for most of the country.

Not everyone changes on the same day

This is where most scheduling mistakes come from. The United States and Canada move their clocks forward on the second Sunday in March and back on the first Sunday in November. The United Kingdom and the European Union change on the last Sunday in March and the last Sunday in October.

So for a few weeks each year the gap between New York and London is four hours instead of the usual five: for two or three weeks in March, and for one week around the end of October. A meeting fixed at 3 pm London time will start an hour earlier than usual for colleagues in New York during those weeks.

The southern hemisphere does it the other way round

South of the equator, summer falls between December and February. Places that use daylight saving time there move their clocks forward in September or October and back in April. Sydney, for example, changes on the first Sunday in October and the first Sunday in April.

Because Sydney and London change in opposite seasons, the difference between them moves between 9, 10 and 11 hours during the year. It is 11 hours in January, 9 hours in July, and 10 hours for a few weeks in between.

Will clock changes end?

Many governments have debated stopping the twice-yearly change. In 2019 the European Parliament voted to end it, but member states have not agreed on how, so clocks in Europe still change. Other countries have already chosen one time and kept it: Türkiye stays on its summer time all year, while Russia went back to permanent standard time in 2014.

How to avoid mistakes

  • Check calendar invitations for recurring meetings in the weeks around March, April, October and November.
  • When you create a meeting, set it in the time zone of the person who chairs it, so the calendar adjusts everyone else's time correctly.
  • For a single date, use the slider on the world clock to see every city at once. Each city page also lists that city's next clock changes.
