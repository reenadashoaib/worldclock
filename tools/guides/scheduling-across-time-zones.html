Scheduling a call between people in different countries means finding hours that fall inside everyone's working day, or deciding who gets the early start or the late finish. A few habits make it much easier.

Start with the overlap

Write down each person's normal working hours in their own time, then convert them to one time zone and look for the hours that overlap. Some common pairs, when both cities are on standard time:

  • London and New York are five hours apart. A 9 am to 5 pm day in New York is 2 pm to 10 pm in London, so the overlap is 2 pm to 5 pm London time: three hours.
  • London and Mumbai are five and a half hours apart in the UK's winter. Mumbai's working day ends at 11:30 am London time, so mornings in London work best. In the UK's summer the gap shrinks to four and a half hours, and the overlap grows by an hour.
  • New York and Tokyo are 14 hours apart in the northern winter, and their working days do not overlap at all. The usual answer is early morning in New York, which is late evening in Tokyo: 8 am in New York is 10 pm in Tokyo.
  • Dubai and Singapore are four hours apart, which leaves a comfortable four-hour overlap in the middle of the day.

Watch the working week

Not every country works Monday to Friday. In Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Israel the working week usually runs from Sunday to Thursday, so Friday is part of the weekend. The United Arab Emirates moved to a Monday-to-Friday week in 2022. For calls with the Gulf, Monday to Thursday is usually the safest choice.

Always name the time zone

"Let's talk at 3 o'clock" means different things to different people. Write the time with its city or offset, for example "3 pm London (UTC+0)", or let your calendar app send an invitation, which converts the time for each person automatically. Avoid abbreviations like CST or IST on their own: several time zones share them. Our guide to UTC, GMT and time zone names explains why.

Plan around clock changes

The United States, Europe and Australia change their clocks on different dates, so a recurring meeting can drift by an hour for a few weeks each spring and autumn. See how daylight saving time works for the dates.

Share the inconvenient slots

When there is no overlap, someone has to join early or stay late. For regular meetings, rotate the time so the same people are not always the ones on the 7 am or 10 pm call. For teams spread across many countries, it often works better to share updates in writing and keep live meetings for decisions.
